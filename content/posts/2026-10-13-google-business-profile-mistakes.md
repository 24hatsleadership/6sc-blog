Someone is on their phone, looking for a business like yours. Before they walk through the door, they look at your Google listing. That listing is your digital storefront, the front window. The useful Google Business Profile tips are mostly about keeping that window clear. These four Google Business Profile mistakes show up again and again, and each one is fixable this week.

The examples are composites, not any one shop. Dan and Kate walk through the same four on Marketing Tune-Up.

## Your listing is the front window

When someone searches nearby, Google shows a few businesses side by side. Photos, hours, reviews, and the phone number are all in that first glance. The listing with a recent photo, the right hours, and reviews that someone answered looks like the safer choice.

The other businesses are not worse. They look less sure from the outside, and that doubt costs walk-ins and bookings.

## Mistake 1: The listing was never claimed

Picture a hair studio with loyal regulars, and nobody who ever took ownership of the Google profile. No phone number, a handful of old photos, and no way for the owner to answer anything. A new customer wants to book and cannot call, so they tap the next salon.

An unclaimed listing means you cannot correct the details, add photos, or reply to reviews. Google may show a profile anyway. You just are not holding the keys.

Search your business name on Google Maps, on your phone, the way a customer would. If you cannot edit the hours, the listing is not yours yet. Claiming it tells Google you are the owner. Verification is how Google confirms that. Do it once. After that, the phone, the hours, the categories, and the photos are yours to fix.

If a second listing of the same business turns up, sort out which one you control before you polish the wrong one. Two profiles split the photos and the reviews.

## Mistake 2: The details are wrong

A boutique moves from one suite to another. A cafe changes its hours for the season. Google still shows the old information. Or the number on the listing is an old line nobody answers. That is a customer who drove over, found the door locked, and probably will not try again.

Wrong details are a trust problem before they are a marketing problem. The person did what you hoped. They showed up. The window told them a story that was not true.

Check the pieces a customer uses to decide, and to arrive:

- The business name is the real name on the door, not a slogan stuffed with search words.
- The address matches where you are this month, including the suite.
- The phone is a number a person answers.
- The website link lands on your site, not on an old page.
- The hours match this week. If a holiday is coming, set the special hours before the long weekend.
- The primary category describes what you actually do. A cafe and a bakery are not the same promise. Pick the one a customer would search.

If any of those are off, fix them today. Then make the same facts match anywhere else you are listed, so you are not asking Google to reconcile three different phone numbers.

## Mistake 3: The profile looks stale

Some listings have reviews and still feel abandoned. The newest photos are a few years old, and nobody has posted in a long time. Think of a pub that redid the patio and printed a new menu, and none of it shows on Google. To someone searching, it looks like nothing changed, or like nobody is paying attention.

Fresh does not mean a photoshoot. It means a current picture of the storefront, the room, or the work, plus a short post about what is new and a description that still matches.

If the newest photo makes you wince, add one this week. That single update says someone is home.

## Mistake 4: Reviews are thin or quiet

A spa has a dozen reviews, and a couple of them never got a reply. Most happy customers would leave a review. They never get asked. The frustrated customer always finds the button.

Thin means a stranger cannot see a pattern yet. Quiet means the reviews sit there unanswered, so the listing feels unattended even when the rating looks fine.

Reply to the ones you have, including the uncomfortable ones. Thank them, or say what you will do. Skip the argument, and skip pasting the same sentence under every review. Then ask a few people who were happy this week, in person or by a normal text. You need the ask, not a system. Unanswered reviews are part of this same window.

## Why these four mistakes matter

People compare a few listings and pick the one that feels safer. Fresh photos, accurate hours, and answered reviews create that feeling. You are not pretending to be a bigger business. You are letting the digital storefront tell the truth about a good one. When the window is dusty, the work inside still gets skipped.

## Do this today

Give it five minutes. On your phone, search your business name on Google Maps and read the listing the way a customer would.

- [ ] Confirm you can edit the listing. If you cannot, start the claim.
- [ ] Check that the phone number is the line you actually answer.
- [ ] Check today's hours, and the rest of this week.
- [ ] Note the date on the newest photo.
- [ ] Scan for a second listing of the same business.
- [ ] If a review has no reply, write one before you close the app.
- [ ] If the newest photo is old enough to bother you, add one new photo this week.

Fix the phone or the hours first if either is wrong. Add the photo next. The reply can be two sentences.
