You can have the right hours and a photo from this month and still lose the person who found you. They saw the listing. Then they decided whether to believe it. That decision runs on reviews.

How many Google reviews do you need? There is no magic number, but there is a small set of findings, and each one has a year on it. Dan and Kate go through them on Marketing Tune-Up. Kate brings the research. Dan is sure eleven reviews are plenty, including one from his aunt. The research is less cozy, and more useful.

If the listing itself is still dusty, start with [the four Google Business Profile mistakes](../google-business-profile-mistakes/). The profile gets you found. The reviews get you chosen.

## People look you up first

Getting found comes before getting picked. According to [BrightLocal's 2023 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2023/), 98% of consumers used the internet to find information about local businesses in 2022.

The digital storefront has to be findable, and then it has to hold up. Reviews are the part of the listing where a stranger hears from other customers, not from you.

## Almost everyone reads them

Finding you and trusting you are different moments. [BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) says 97% of consumers read reviews for local businesses.

Those reviews are the first conversation a stranger has with your business, and you are not in the room. You cannot explain a rough review unless you already replied, and you cannot point at happy regulars unless they wrote it down.

## A few reviews used to feel like enough

This next figure needs a date, or it gets misused. In [BrightLocal's 2015 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey-2015/), most people formed an opinion after reading just 1 to 6 reviews.

> That 1-to-6 figure is older, historical data from 2015. It is not a current target.

Dan hears "six" and relaxes. In 2015, a short stack of reviews did settle the question for most people. It does not describe how people read reviews now, and it is a bad number to aim at. Keep it as history. The 2026 survey is the one that should set the habit.

## Trust has to be earned

In [BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/), 49% of consumers trust online reviews as much as a personal recommendation.

Nearly half. Not a rubber stamp. Everyone else is looking for proof the reviews are real. From the outside, real looks like a pattern: different people, specific details, recent dates, and a business that answers. One loyal review can be sincere. Dan's aunt meant hers. It is not a plan.

## The line a lot of shoppers draw is 20

[BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found that 47% of consumers won't use a business with fewer than 20 reviews.

Twenty reviews do not guarantee the job. The finding says nearly half of people will pass on a business that has not reached the line. Eleven good reviews can still be good, and they can still be why those people never call.

If you are under 20, skip the weekend blast. A few happy customers every week crosses the line and still looks believable. If you are already past 20, the next finding is the one that matters.

## Reviews go stale

[BrightLocal's 2026 Local Consumer Review Survey](https://www.brightlocal.com/research/local-consumer-review-survey/) found that 74% of consumers only care about reviews written in the last three months.

That is BrightLocal's 2026 wording. Older reviews are not fake. They are weak evidence of what you are like now. A long list that went quiet can lose to a shorter list from this month, because that 74% is only weighing the recent ones. You want the volume, and you want a heartbeat.

The useful lesson is the one Dan lands on once he stops defending the eleven. It is not "get reviews." It is "keep getting reviews," including the week everyone got busy and forgot to ask.

## The profile and the reviews are one system

The Google Business Profile gets you found: the name, the hours, the photos, the category, and a listing that looks alive. Reviews get you chosen. A spotless profile with silent reviews looks unfinished. A pile of reviews next to the wrong hours sends people to a locked door.

If you do one thing after this, do not pick a side. Check the date on the newest review, and check that the hours beside it are still true.

## What the ongoing work looks like

You can do this yourself. Asking and replying do not require a project. If the habit keeps falling off the list, we are happy to take it off your hands.

Profile work starts with a cleanup: claim or verify the listing, then fix the name, address, phone, hours, and categories so they match everywhere you are listed. Each month, keep it active with photos, posts about what is new, and a reply to every review.

Maps work is the set of things that matter when someone nearby searches for what you do: categories, listings that agree with each other, and steady activity. We track where you show up and put it in a plain-English report. Nobody honest can promise the top spot. You should be able to see the work.

Review work is a simple ask to happy customers right after the visit, by text or email, so fresh reviews keep coming in. You own all of it. Use one of those pieces, or all of them, or none of them.

## Do this today

Open your business on Google Maps and find the date on the newest review.

- [ ] If that review is older than three months, write down three happy customers from this week.
- [ ] Ask them. A normal message is enough. Say it helps, and send the link.
- [ ] Skip the person who would say yes out of loyalty alone. Ask someone who can describe the visit.
- [ ] Reply to the newest review you already have, even in two sentences.
- [ ] If the total is under 20, ask three people on the same day each week. Keep the weekly ask after you pass 20, so the dates stay recent.
- [ ] Check the hours on that same screen before you close it.

The businesses that look trustworthy in three months are the ones that kept asking, not the ones that collected a pile and stopped.
